# 7. Rules by category

## A. Responsibility

Duties toward people, the environment and the work itself.

| ID | Name | Strength | IF (condition) | THEN (action) | BECAUSE (reason) | Example (software/infra) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="p1"></a>P1 | Public safety first | MUST | always | put public safety above cost, schedule and convenience | failures harm people outside the project | block a release that leaks user data |
| <a id="p2"></a>P2 | Work within competence | MUST | always | work only within your competence, and involve an expert outside it | error rates rise outside known areas | a security engineer reviews an IAM design |
| <a id="p3"></a>P3 | Sustainability | SHOULD | always | reduce environmental impact and resource use across the whole lifecycle | NSPE canon 1 includes sustainable development | scale idle environments to zero |
| <a id="p4"></a>P4 | Stop authority | SHOULD | anyone finds a problem that could cause harm | let that person stop the work without needing permission and without being blamed; the stop must always be available, must override all other functions, and resetting it must not restart the system | the person nearest to a problem sees it first | a deploy freeze that overrides Argo CD auto-sync; lifting the freeze requires an explicit sync |
| <a id="p5"></a>P5 | Psychological safety | SHOULD | people work together on a system | make it safe to report errors, ask questions and disagree: leaders ask for input, thank people for bad news, and never punish an honest report | psychological safety enables learning behavior, which drives team performance; problems that people hide cannot be fixed | the first person to report a broken pipeline is thanked, not blamed; the team lead names their own mistake in the retrospective |
| <a id="p6"></a>P6 | Conflict-of-interest disclosure | MUST | a person decides on, reviews or advises on work in which they have a personal interest | disclose the interest and step out of the decision or review | a personal interest biases judgment and destroys trust in the decision | an engineer who holds shares in a monitoring vendor leaves its evaluation |
| <a id="p7"></a>P7 | Commander's intent | SHOULD | you delegate a task | state the purpose, the desired end state and the constraints, and leave the method to the people who do the work (mission orders) | plans meet changing conditions; people who know the purpose can act correctly without new orders | an epic states "why", "done when" and "must not", not a list of kubectl steps |
| <a id="p8"></a>P8 | Fair process | SHOULD | a decision affects people | engage them (ask for input and challenge), explain the final decision and its reasons, and state the new expectations clearly; this is not consensus | people accept and carry out decisions they disagree with when the process was fair | before changing the on-call model, the team is asked, then told the decision, the reasons and the new rules |

| ID | Class (why) | HOW TO CHECK | VIOLATION (opposite of the rule) | RELATED | Evidence | Source domain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [P1](#p1) | universal (U), category A: true for every system | every release gate contains a safety and security sign-off item | shipping a known safety defect to meet a deadline | strengthens [P37](#p37), [P38](#p38); strengthened by [P4](#p4) | strong | all (professional ethics) |
| [P2](#p2) | universal (U), category A: true for every system | each design has a named checker with expertise in its domain | approving work in a domain you do not know | strengthens [P56](#p56); strengthened by [P6](#p6), [P62](#p62) | strong | all (professional ethics) |
| [P3](#p3) | universal (U), category A: true for every system | resource use is measured and reviewed | unused resources running all the time | depends on [P24](#p24); strengthened by [P24](#p24), [P49](#p49) | moderate | all (professional ethics) |
| [P4](#p4) | universal (U), category A: trigger that occurs in the life cycle of every system | the stop mechanism exists, works in every operating mode, does not restart on reset, and every use is reviewed without blame | a release continues while a known problem is still being discussed | depends on [P80](#p80); strengthens [P1](#p1), [P82](#p82); strengthened by [P5](#p5) | strong (emergency stop is standardized in ISO 13850) | manufacturing (Toyota andon), machinery (emergency stop) |
| [P5](#p5) | universal (U), category A: every system is built or operated by people who work together | a regular anonymous survey item on safety to speak up; the number of reported near misses does not fall after an incident | punishing the person who reported a problem | strengthens [P80](#p80), [P82](#p82), [P4](#p4); strengthened by [P63](#p63); conflicts with accountability (see [K7](08-conflicts.md#k7)) | strong (peer-reviewed field study; Google study of 180 teams) | management (team leadership) |
| [P6](#p6) | universal (U), category A: trigger that occurs in the life cycle of every system (every system has suppliers, reviewers and decision-makers) | a register of declared interests exists and is checked before each vendor selection, design review and approval | a reviewer approves a design that uses a product of a company they own | strengthens [P2](#p2), [P43](#p43), [P56](#p56) | strong (NSPE Code of Ethics II.4.a; G20/OECD Principles II.F.2) | professional ethics, management (corporate governance) |
| [P7](#p7) | universal (U), category A: every engineering organization delegates work | each delegated task or epic states purpose, end state and constraints | a step list with no purpose; work stops when the plan no longer fits | depends on [P2](#p2); conflicts with [P71](#p71), [P72](#p72), [P61](#p61) (see [K6](08-conflicts.md#k6)) | strong (US Army doctrine ADP 6-0) | management (military command and control) |
| [P8](#p8) | universal (U), category A: every engineering organization makes decisions that affect people | for each decision that changes people's work: an input round, a written explanation and stated expectations exist | announcing a reorganization without reasons | strengthens [P64](#p64), [P69](#p69) | moderate (management research; practitioner article) | management (organizational justice) |

## B. Knowledge and decisions

How requirements, decisions, models and evidence are recorded and trusted.

| ID | Name | Strength | IF (condition) | THEN (action) | BECAUSE (reason) | Example (software/infra) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="p9"></a>P9 | Measurable requirements | MUST | always | write each requirement as measurable, and link it to a user need | an unmeasurable requirement cannot be verified | "p95 latency \< 200 ms", linked to a ticket |
| <a id="p10"></a>P10 | Decision records | SHOULD | you make a significant design decision | record the decision, why you made it, and which alternatives you rejected | without the reason, later teams cannot judge whether the decision still holds | Architecture Decision Record (ADR) in the repository |
| <a id="p11"></a>P11 | Trustworthy evidence | SHOULD | a record is used as evidence (test result, approval, measurement) | make it attributable, legible, recorded at the time, original and accurate; changes must keep the original entry visible and record who, when and why | evidence that can be changed silently proves nothing | signed commits, immutable audit logs, CI results stored with the commit SHA |
| <a id="p12"></a>P12 | Model validity | SHOULD | a decision depends on a model, simulation or estimate | validate the model for its intended use, use it only inside the range it was validated for (domain of applicability), and check each result against an independent rough estimate (order of magnitude) | a model is only trustworthy inside its validated range | a load test on 3 nodes does not prove behavior on 50 nodes |
| <a id="p13"></a>P13 | Automated reproducible results | SHOULD | a result must be reproducible | produce it only through automated, recorded steps, with versions of data, code and tools | manual steps are error-prone and cannot be reproduced | infrastructure changed only through pipelines; no manual kubectl edits; pinned tool versions |
| <a id="p14"></a>P14 | Provenance | SHOULD | parts or materials come from suppliers | trace each part to its origin, batch and certificate (provenance) | without provenance you cannot prove quality, or find all affected parts when a defect is found | signed images with build provenance and SBOM; deployment by pinned digest |
| <a id="p15"></a>P15 | Precaution | SHOULD | a possible harm is serious or irreversible, and the evidence is incomplete | take proportionate, cost-effective protective measures without waiting for full proof | irreversible harm cannot be undone once the proof arrives | take a snapshot and stop writes when data corruption is suspected, before the root cause is proven |
| <a id="p16"></a>P16 | Set-based design | SHOULD | uncertainty is high early in a design | develop several alternatives in parallel, eliminate weak ones as evidence arrives, and commit only after feasibility is shown (set-based design; opposite: point-based design) | committing early to one option risks expensive rework when it fails | prototype two ingress controllers until load tests decide |
| <a id="p17"></a>P17 | Technology maturity | SHOULD | the design depends on a technology that is new to the team or the industry | assess how mature the technology is, and keep immature technology out of critical paths | immature technology carries unknown failures | CNCF sandbox project vs. graduated project for a core platform component |
| <a id="p18"></a>P18 | Decision speed by reversibility | SHOULD | you make a decision | classify it as reversible (two-way door) or irreversible (one-way door); make reversible decisions quickly at the lowest competent level, and irreversible decisions slowly, with consultation and review | heavy process on reversible decisions causes slowness and too little experimentation; light process on irreversible decisions causes lasting harm | a new linter rule: the team decides today; deleting a production database or changing the cloud provider: [P42](#p42), red team, ADR |
| <a id="p19"></a>P19 | Indicators are not targets | SHOULD | a measure is used to reward, rank or punish people | expect it to be gamed: pair it with a counter-measure, compare it with direct observation, and change it when it drifts from the real objective | the more a quantitative indicator is used for decisions, the more it is corrupted and the more it distorts the process it measures (Campbell's law) | deployment frequency is paired with change failure rate; MTTR is not a bonus target |
| <a id="p20"></a>P20 | Stop criteria set in advance | SHOULD | you start a project or prototype with an uncertain outcome | write down, before starting, the evidence and the decision points (gates) at which it stops, and let someone who did not start it make the continue-or-stop decision | people who are responsible for an earlier decision commit the most resources to it after negative results (escalation of commitment) | a service-mesh pilot stops if p99 latency rises by more than 5 ms after 4 weeks; the architecture board, not the sponsor, decides |
| <a id="p21"></a>P21 | Specific, challenging goals with feedback | SHOULD | you set goals for people or teams | set few, specific and challenging goals, give regular feedback on progress toward them, and for new or complex tasks set learning goals first | specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than "do your best", and goals plus feedback work better than goals alone | "cut median build time from 12 to 6 minutes this quarter", with a weekly chart; for a new technology: "run three spikes and document what we learned" |

| ID | Class (why) | HOW TO CHECK | VIOLATION (opposite of the rule) | RELATED | Evidence | Source domain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [P9](#p9) | universal (U), category B: true for every system | every requirement has an acceptance criterion and a link to a user need | "the system shall be fast" | strengthens [P54](#p54), [P59](#p59); strengthened by [P21](#p21) | moderate | systems engineering |
| [P10](#p10) | universal (U), category B: trigger that occurs in the life cycle of every system | each significant decision has a record with its reason and the rejected alternatives | nobody knows why the system is built this way | strengthens [P64](#p64), [P80](#p80); strengthened by [P16](#p16), [P18](#p18), [P20](#p20) | moderate | software (ADR), civil and offshore (basis of design) |
| [P11](#p11) | universal (U), category B: trigger that occurs in the life cycle of every system | a sample of records shows author, time and full change history | editing a test report after the fact without a trace | strengthens [P59](#p59), [P64](#p64); strengthened by [P13](#p13), [P14](#p14) | strong (required in regulated domains) | pharma and clinical trials (ALCOA) |
| [P12](#p12) | universal (U), category B: every design decision rests on a model or an estimate | each model used for a decision states its validated range, and the decision lies inside that range | extrapolating a benchmark far beyond the tested load | depends on [P54](#p54); strengthens [P44](#p44); strengthened by [P34](#p34), [P58](#p58), [P77](#p77) | moderate | simulation and computational engineering, engineering physics (Fermi estimates) |
| [P13](#p13) | universal (U), category B: every engineering result must be checkable by someone else ([P56](#p56)) | each result can be regenerated from recorded inputs and versions with one command | a report built by copying numbers by hand | depends on [P64](#p64); strengthens [P11](#p11) | moderate | bioinformatics (reproducible computational research) |
| [P14](#p14) | universal (U), category B: almost every system uses parts or software from suppliers | for each deployed artifact, origin, build and certificate can be shown | running an unsigned image from an unknown registry | strengthens [P11](#p11), [P78](#p78) | moderate | metallurgical engineering (mill test certificates) |
| [P15](#p15) | risk-scaled (R), category B: only for serious or irreversible harm with incomplete evidence | for each serious or irreversible risk with incomplete evidence, a record states which protective measure was taken, or why none was | writes continue while the team waits for proof of data corruption | strengthens [P37](#p37), [P82](#p82); strengthened by [P18](#p18) | moderate (contested: critics call it vague and say strong versions block beneficial technology; this rule uses the weak, proportionate version) | environmental engineering (precautionary principle) |
| [P16](#p16) | risk-scaled (R), category B: only where uncertainty is high early; for a repeat design, one option is enough | early design records list the alternatives kept and the evidence that eliminated each rejected one | choosing one design at the start and discovering at the end that it cannot meet a requirement | depends on [P58](#p58); strengthens [P10](#p10), [P17](#p17); strengthened by [P20](#p20) | moderate | design engineering (Toyota set-based concurrent engineering) |
| [P17](#p17) | conditional (C), category B: counterexample: a system built only from proven technology | each technology in a critical path has a recorded maturity assessment | adopting an untested tool for the production control plane without an assessment | depends on [P10](#p10); strengthens [P22](#p22); strengthened by [P16](#p16), [P20](#p20) | strong (standardized as ISO 16290:2013; NASA defines TRL 1 to 9) | aerospace (NASA TRL), EU, DoD |
| [P18](#p18) | universal (U), category B: trigger that occurs in the life cycle of every system | each decision record ([P10](#p10)) states the class (reversible or irreversible) and the matching process | a three-week approval for a reversible tool trial; a lock-in contract signed in one meeting | strengthens [P10](#p10), [P15](#p15), [P42](#p42); conflicts with [P56](#p56), [P64](#p64) (see [K5](08-conflicts.md#k5)) | moderate (practitioner source: a CEO's shareholder letter; consistent with [P15](#p15) and [P42](#p42)) | management (decision making) |
| [P19](#p19) | universal (U), category B: every system and every team whose performance is measured ([P70](#p70), [P58](#p58)) | each rewarded indicator has a counter-indicator and a yearly review of whether it still tracks the objective | ticket count as the only measure of on-call quality | strengthens [P58](#p58), [P70](#p70), [P82](#p82); conflicts with [P21](#p21) | strong (Campbell; the same pattern is known as Goodhart's law) | management (performance measurement) |
| [P20](#p20) | universal (U), category B: trigger that occurs in the life cycle of every system (every system starts as a project) | each project and prototype has written stop criteria and gate dates, and gate decisions are made by someone other than the sponsor | "we have invested too much to stop now" | strengthens [P10](#p10), [P16](#p16), [P17](#p17), [P65](#p65) | strong (experimental research, Staw 1976; PRINCE2 "continued business justification") | management (project management, organizational psychology) |
| [P21](#p21) | universal (U), category B: every engineering organization sets goals | each goal has a measure, a deadline and a progress chart; new or complex work has learning goals | "do your best" as the only goal; goals without progress feedback | strengthens [P9](#p9); strengthened by [P63](#p63); conflicts with [P19](#p19) (see [K8](08-conflicts.md#k8)) | strong (experimental and field research summarized in the paper); contested by Deming points 11 and 12, who rejects management by numerical goals | management (goals and motivation (goal-setting theory)) |

## C. Design

How to shape a design before it is built, so that it fails less.

| ID | Name | Strength | IF (condition) | THEN (action) | BECAUSE (reason) | Example (software/infra) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="p22"></a>P22 | Failure and interaction analysis | SHOULD | always | before you build, list how each part can fail, how parts that have not failed can interact unsafely, and how the system can cause harm while working as designed (insufficient design or foreseeable misuse) | a known failure or interaction can be designed against | "what happens if the registry is down?" and "what if two controllers fight over the same resource?" |
| <a id="p23"></a>P23 | Simplicity and modularity | SHOULD | always | choose the simplest design that meets the requirements, and split it into modules | every added part adds a way to fail | one Helm chart per app, not one chart for all |
| <a id="p24"></a>P24 | Lifecycle and decommissioning | SHOULD | always | plan operation, maintenance and decommissioning at design time | early design decisions fix most end-of-life costs | define how to delete a cluster and its data before creating it |
| <a id="p25"></a>P25 | Human factors | SHOULD | a person uses or operates the system | design the interface for the real user in the real environment, including stress, and for the full range of users, not an average user | a poor interface causes errors | a runbook that works at 3 a.m.; unambiguous CLI output |
| <a id="p26"></a>P26 | Design for testability | SHOULD | always | build ways to set and observe the internal state of each part into the design, so that it can be tested | a state you cannot set or observe cannot be tested | health and readiness endpoints, dry-run modes, feature flags for testing, debug endpoints |
| <a id="p27"></a>P27 | Robustness | SHOULD | always | design so that the result changes little when inputs vary (robustness) | removing every cause of variation costs more than tolerating it | retries with jitter, idempotent operations, tolerance for clock drift |
| <a id="p28"></a>P28 | Accumulated degradation | SHOULD | always | design for the damage that builds up over the whole design life, not only for the single largest load | repeated loads far below the maximum load cause failure, and the damage does not recover | soak tests over days, not only peak tests; disk wear; leaks that grow with uptime |
| <a id="p29"></a>P29 | Compatibility | SHOULD | parts of different kinds touch or interact | verify that they are compatible with each other in the real environment, and separate them where they are not | incompatible parts damage each other, even when each part is good on its own | tested compatibility matrix for Kubernetes, CNI and CSI versions; separate node pools for incompatible kernel modules |
| <a id="p30"></a>P30 | Exact constraint | SHOULD | several mechanisms can set the same property | constrain each property exactly once, with one owner (exact constraint) | over-constraint makes parts fight each other and deform the system | either the HPA or the GitOps manifest owns the replica count, not both; one source of truth per configuration value |
| <a id="p31"></a>P31 | Design by selection | SHOULD | no model is good enough to design the solution analytically | generate many variants, test them, keep the best, and repeat (design by selection) | selection by test finds good solutions without knowing exactly how they work | A/B tests, fuzzing, parameter search for autoscaler settings |
| <a id="p32"></a>P32 | End-to-end | SHOULD | a function (integrity, security, deduplication) can only be fully correct with knowledge at the end points | implement it at the end points; add it in lower layers only to improve performance | lower layers cannot guarantee the function and would burden every user | mTLS between services instead of relying only on network encryption; checksums on backups at the application level |
| <a id="p33"></a>P33 | Strict interfaces | SHOULD | components exchange data over an interface | send strictly according to the specification, reject and report input that violates it instead of silently accepting it, and state units, time zone and reference frames explicitly in every interface | silently tolerated defects become permanent unofficial standards and security holes, and unstated units get misread | schema validation in CI; fail on unknown fields; field names like latency_ms; timestamps in UTC with offset |
| <a id="p34"></a>P34 | Weakest-link scaling | SHOULD | a system grows in size or in number of parts | expect rare failures to become frequent, and design and test for the target scale | the larger the system, the more likely it contains a critical weak part (weakest link) | at 1,000 nodes, a node failure that happens once in 1,000 days per node happens every day |
| <a id="p35"></a>P35 | Structure follows the intended system | SHOULD | an organization designs a system with several components | design team boundaries and communication paths to match the component structure you want, and change them when the intended structure changes | organizations produce designs that copy their own communication structure (Conway's law) | one team per bounded context; the platform team owns the platform API, not parts of each product's code |

| ID | Class (why) | HOW TO CHECK | VIOLATION (opposite of the rule) | RELATED | Evidence | Source domain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [P22](#p22) | universal (U), category C: true for every system | a list of failure modes and unsafe interactions exists per component and is reviewed | the first failure analysis happens after an incident | strengthens [P37](#p37), [P38](#p38), [P41](#p41), [P44](#p44), [P45](#p45), [P46](#p46); strengthened by [P17](#p17), [P29](#p29), [P55](#p55) | moderate (method widely used; RPN scoring criticized) | reliability and safety (FMEA, STPA), automotive (SOTIF) |
| [P23](#p23) | universal (U), category C: true for every system | each component has one stated purpose, and no part is unused | adding features for imagined future needs | strengthened by [P24](#p24), [P30](#p30), [P35](#p35); conflicts with [P40](#p40), [P41](#p41), [P44](#p44), [P45](#p45), [P46](#p46), [P47](#p47) | moderate | aerospace (Lockheed Skunk Works), software |
| [P24](#p24) | universal (U), category C: true for every system | each design document has a maintenance and a decommissioning section | orphaned resources that nobody can safely remove | strengthens [P3](#p3), [P23](#p23); strengthened by [P78](#p78) | moderate | nuclear, offshore, aerospace |
| [P25](#p25) | universal (U), category C: trigger that occurs in the life cycle of every system | critical tasks are tested with real users or operators | blaming users for errors that the interface invites | depends on [P80](#p80); strengthens [P39](#p39); strengthened by [P71](#p71), [P76](#p76) | moderate (strong in medical devices) | human factors, medical devices, biomechanics (anthropometry) |
| [P26](#p26) | universal (U), category C: true for every system | each component has documented test access to set and observe its key states | a component that can only be tested in production | strengthens [P54](#p54), [P57](#p57), [P70](#p70) | moderate | computer engineering (design for testing) |
| [P27](#p27) | universal (U), category C: every system meets variation in its inputs | tests with varied inputs (latency, clock drift, message order) show stable results | a system that works only when timing is exact | depends on [P22](#p22); strengthens [P44](#p44); conflicts with [P33](#p33) | moderate | manufacturing quality (Taguchi), agricultural engineering |
| [P28](#p28) | universal (U), category C: every system that is used more than once degrades over time | an endurance (soak) test runs for a period that represents real use, and the growth of leaks, wear or fragmentation is measured | only a short peak test, then failure after weeks of normal load | depends on [P22](#p22); strengthens [P74](#p74) | moderate | materials, mechanical, piping (fatigue, corrosion allowance) |
| [P29](#p29) | universal (U), category C: every system with parts of different kinds has interactions between them | each interacting pair of components is on a tested compatibility list | upgrading one component and breaking another that was never tested with it | strengthens [P22](#p22), [P33](#p33) | moderate | materials (galvanic corrosion), biomaterials (biocompatibility) |
| [P30](#p30) | universal (U), category C: over-constraint can occur in any system with more than one control mechanism | each managed field has exactly one owner | Argo CD and the HPA both set the replica count and keep reverting each other | strengthens [P23](#p23), [P64](#p64); strengthened by [P35](#p35), [P79](#p79) | moderate | optomechanical and precision engineering (kinematic coupling) |
| [P31](#p31) | risk-scaled (R), category C: only where models are too weak for analytical design | each selection round has a defined test and records which variants were kept | choosing a configuration by opinion when it could be tested | depends on [P58](#p58); strengthens [P81](#p81) | moderate (directed evolution received the 2018 Nobel Prize in Chemistry) | molecular and biomolecular engineering (directed evolution, DBTL) |
| [P32](#p32) | conditional (C), category C: counterexample: a system without communication between end points (a passive structure) | for each end-to-end property (integrity, authentication, exactly-once), the end points verify it themselves | trusting the network to deliver data correctly without a check in the application | strengthens [P40](#p40) | moderate | network engineering (Saltzer, Reed, Clark 1984) |
| [P33](#p33) | conditional (C), category C: counterexample: a system without data interfaces (a passive structure) | every interface has a machine-checked schema, and invalid input is rejected and logged | silently ignoring unknown or malformed fields | strengthens [P39](#p39), [P82](#p82); strengthened by [P29](#p29), [P35](#p35); conflicts with [P27](#p27) | moderate (disputed: Postel's original principle says to accept liberally; RFC 9413 argues against it) | network protocol engineering (Postel, RFC 9413), space systems (Mars Climate Orbiter units) |
| [P34](#p34) | conditional (C), category C: counterexample: a system of fixed, small size | failure rates are estimated per component and multiplied by the planned scale | assuming that a failure never seen in a 3-node test will not happen at 300 nodes | strengthens [P12](#p12), [P45](#p45) | strong (ASTM C1683: "The larger the specimen or component, the weaker it is likely to be"; standard text is paid; only the official summary was read) | ceramics and brittle materials (Weibull statistics, size effect) |
| [P35](#p35) | conditional (C), category C: counterexample: a system built by one person, or by one small team that communicates as one unit | each major component boundary has one owning team, and cross-team dependencies are counted | one component maintained by four teams that never talk to each other | strengthens [P23](#p23), [P30](#p30), [P33](#p33) | moderate (widely observed; one original paper) | management (organization design), software engineering |

## D. Protection

Controls that prevent harm, or contain it when it happens.

| ID | Name | Strength | IF (condition) | THEN (action) | BECAUSE (reason) | Example (software/infra) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="p36"></a>P36 | Least privilege | MUST | always | give each person, service and process only the minimum access its function needs | minimum access limits the damage when one part is compromised | Kubernetes RBAC scoped to one namespace |
| <a id="p37"></a>P37 | Fail-safe | MUST | always | define a safe state, and design failure to move there (fail-safe; opposite: fail-dangerous) | an uncontrolled failure causes the most harm | admission webhook rejects requests when it errors (fail-closed; opposite: fail-open) |
| <a id="p38"></a>P38 | Hierarchy of controls | SHOULD | a risk is found | use the highest possible control level: 1 eliminate; 2 minimize, substitute or moderate; 3 engineering control; 4 administrative control; 5 protective equipment; and act first at the source, then along the path, then at the receiver | higher control levels do not depend on human behavior, and a cause removed at the source cannot spread | 1 remove the port → 2 store less data / safer library / lower rate limit → 3 policy engine → 4 runbook → 5 "be careful"; noisy neighbor: limit the source → separate node pool → client timeouts |
| <a id="p39"></a>P39 | Error-proofing | SHOULD | a person can make the same error repeatedly | make the error impossible; if that is not possible, make the error visible immediately; if that is not possible either, limit its consequence so that it is survivable | errors are certain, defects are not | branch protection and required CI checks (impossible); a dry-run diff (visible); soft delete with a restore window (survivable) |
| <a id="p40"></a>P40 | Defense in depth | SHOULD | always | use several independent and verified control layers (defense in depth) | every single control fails at some point | RBAC + network policy + admission policy + runtime isolation |
| <a id="p41"></a>P41 | Compartments | SHOULD | always | divide the system into isolated compartments, so that one damaged compartment does not take down the whole system | a failure that cannot spread stays small | separate cloud accounts per environment, namespace quotas, cell-based architecture, bulkhead pattern |
| <a id="p42"></a>P42 | Two-person rule | SHOULD | an action is critical and cannot be undone | require two authorized people at the moment of action (two-person rule) | one person can make an error or act with bad intent, and nobody stops it | break-glass production access and deletion of production data need a second approver, live |
| <a id="p43"></a>P43 | Segregation of duties | MUST | a process handles money, assets, access rights or records, and the possible loss is above the acceptable risk | split the process so that no single person can request, approve, execute and record the same transaction; where the team is too small, add a compensating control (a later independent review) | a person who controls every step can cause an error or fraud and hide it | the engineer who requests production access cannot approve it; nobody can grant themselves admin rights and also delete the audit log |

## E. Capacity and continuity

How the system keeps working under high load and partial failure.

| ID | Name | Strength | IF (condition) | THEN (action) | BECAUSE (reason) | Example (software/infra) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="p44"></a>P44 | Margin | SHOULD | always | design capacity above the expected load (margin), state the design load as a probability per time period, keep queued resources well below 100 % utilization, and set the margin by design maturity: larger early, smaller as uncertainty falls | real load exceeds estimated load, and delay rises sharply as utilization approaches 100 % | capacity for the 1-in-100-day traffic peak plus 30 % headroom; worker pools sized for \< 70 % average utilization |
| <a id="p45"></a>P45 | Redundancy (N-1) | SHOULD | one failure must not stop the function | add redundancy, so that the function survives the loss of any single element (N-1), and make sure each copy and the switch-over mechanism are reliable enough for the redundancy to help | one copy is a single point of failure | nodes in several availability zones |
| <a id="p46"></a>P46 | Diversity | SHOULD | one cause can hit all copies at once | make the copies different (diversity) | identical copies fail together | two different DNS providers |
| <a id="p47"></a>P47 | Graceful degradation | SHOULD | demand can exceed capacity | define in advance which lower-priority functions switch off first, so that critical functions keep running (graceful degradation; opposite: cascading failure) | uncontrolled overload makes the whole system collapse | PriorityClasses with preemption, rate limiting, switching off recommendations to keep checkout running |
| <a id="p48"></a>P48 | Damping | SHOULD | a control loop reacts automatically to measurements | add a deadband, delay or cooldown, so that the loop does not switch back and forth (oscillation; opposite: stable control) | repeated on-off cycles waste resources and destabilize the system | HPA stabilization window; failover hysteresis; circuit breaker cooldown |
| <a id="p49"></a>P49 | Induced demand | SHOULD | you add capacity to relieve congestion | expect demand to grow into the new capacity, and also manage demand (quotas, priorities, pricing) | added capacity lowers the cost of use, which increases use | a larger cluster fills up again; faster CI leads to more CI runs |
| <a id="p50"></a>P50 | Bottleneck first | SHOULD | you want more throughput | find the constraint (bottleneck) and improve it first | improving other parts does not increase output | speeding up lint does nothing if integration tests or the review queue are the bottleneck |
| <a id="p51"></a>P51 | Shared demand signal | SHOULD | several stages pass demand signals along a chain | share the real end demand with every stage, instead of letting each stage react only to the stage below it | each stage amplifies the variation it sees, so swings grow upstream (bullwhip effect) | upstream services scale on front-end traffic, not only on their own delayed queue length |
| <a id="p52"></a>P52 | Limit work in progress | SHOULD | a team or system handles a flow of work items | set a limit on the number of items in progress, and finish items before starting new ones | at a given throughput, average lead time is proportional to average work in progress (Little's law), so more parallel work means longer waiting for every item | at most 4 epics in progress per team; at most N concurrent rollouts per cluster |
| <a id="p53"></a>P53 | Late work and added people | SHOULD | work is late, and it needs coordination and learning | do not expect added people to recover the schedule soon; cut or move scope first, and add people early, only where the work can be split | new people need training and add communication paths, which at first lowers the output of the existing team (Brooks's law) | two weeks before a cluster-migration deadline, scope is cut; new engineers join the next migration from its start |

## G. Change

How to change the system safely.

| ID | Name | Strength | IF (condition) | THEN (action) | BECAUSE (reason) | Example (software/infra) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="p64"></a>P64 | Versioning and change review | MUST | always | version every artifact; before each change, review its technical basis, its safety impact and the procedures it affects, approve it, and inform the affected people before go-live | an unknown version makes failures unreproducible, and an unreviewed change brings in unknown hazards | GitOps: every change is a reviewed pull request; the runbook is updated in the same pull request |
| <a id="p65"></a>P65 | Small verified steps | SHOULD | a change carries risk | make the change in small steps, check the result of each step before the next one, and define each check as a hold point (work stops until the check is approved) or a witness point (work continues while the check is observed) | small steps limit how much harm one wrong step can do | canary rollout at 1 % → 10 % → 100 %, with a manual approval gate (hold point) before 100 % |

| <a id="p72"></a>P72 | Critical control points | SHOULD | a repeated process has a few steps where control is essential | define those critical control points, rank them by consequence rather than by how often incidents happen, and give each one a limit, monitoring, a predefined corrective action, a record and regular verification in practice | tight control at the essential steps works better than loose control at every step | image-signature admission gate; SLO limit on the payment path with automatic rollback |

| <a id="p78"></a>P78 | Asset inventory | SHOULD | you operate many assets | keep a complete inventory of all assets, with owner, condition and lifecycle stage | you cannot maintain, secure or decommission what you do not know you have | inventory of clusters, accounts, certificates and images with owners; SBOM per image |

| [P75](#p75) | universal (U), category H: every operated system is monitored ([P70](#p70)), and monitoring raises alerts | each alert has an owner and a runbook action, and the share of alerts with no action is measured and kept low | an alert channel with hundreds of messages per day that nobody reads | strengthens [P70](#p70), [P73](#p73), [P82](#p82) | strong (The Joint Commission: "between 85 and 99 percent of alarm signals do not require clinical intervention") | clinical engineering, rail dispatch (alarm fatigue) |

| [P78](#p78) | conditional (C), category H: counterexample: a single, small asset | an automated discovery compares the real assets with the inventory and finds no unknown assets | a forgotten test cluster with public access | strengthens [P24](#p24), [P36](#p36), [P74](#p74); strengthened by [P14](#p14) | moderate | municipal and infrastructure engineering (asset management, ISO 55000) |
