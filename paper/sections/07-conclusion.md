# Conclusion {#sec-conclusion}

Engineering practice is published per domain, but many practices are
shared across domains. This paper has stated 83 of them as conditional
rules in one sentence pattern, with a check, a typical violation, related
rules, an evidence rating and sources for each, and has added 8 conflict
rules for the cases where two rules pull in opposite directions. A
classification test built on the criteria for systems engineering
principles [@incose-se-principles; @sebok-se-principles] sorts the rules
into 58 universal, 9 risk-scaled and 16 conditional rules, and a single
master rule, together with a rigor matrix, says how strictly each rule
applies to a given system. Rules from engineering sources are also stated
in management form, and 16 rules come from management sources, so that one
taxonomy covers both the system and the organization that builds it,
and the nine categories double as a nine-question checklist.

The taxonomy positions itself between principles, which deliberately avoid
how-to statements, and process standards and handbooks, which describe
processes within one discipline or agency
[@iso-iec-ieee-15288-2023; @nasa-se-handbook-rev2]. Its distinctive features
are that each practice names the condition under which it applies, that
each conditional rule names a system where it does not, and that the
support for each rule, including where that support is secondary, paid or
contested, is visible on the rule itself.

The result is a synthesis, not an empirical study. Its evidence ratings
measure institutional endorsement rather than measured effect, its
classification and its management transfers are the author's judgments,
and its coverage rests on a saturation argument that can only predict that
few rules remain unfound [@saunders-2018-saturation]. These limits are
stated with the sources, and the method that produced the taxonomy is
described so that each of them can be checked or reduced by further work.
The taxonomy is released as an openly licensed, machine-checked dataset
from which the published tables and this paper's results are generated, so
that later editions can extend it without losing what is already sourced.
