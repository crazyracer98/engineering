# Abstract {.unnumbered}

Engineering disciplines each publish their own codes, handbooks and lessons
learned, but the practices they share are rarely stated once, in a form that
can be checked, with the evidence for each practice made explicit. This
paper presents a cross-domain taxonomy of 83 engineering practices written as
conditional rules (IF a condition holds, THEN take an action, BECAUSE of a
stated reason), together with 8 conflict rules that decide between rules that
pull in opposite directions. The rules are sorted into 9 categories that
follow the life cycle of an engineered system, from responsibility to
learning, and each category is condensed into one question, so the taxonomy
doubles as a nine-item checklist. A classification test adapted
from the criteria for systems engineering principles [@incose-se-principles;
@sebok-se-principles] separates 58 universal rules, 9 risk-scaled rules and 16
conditional rules, each conditional rule naming a counterexample system. A
master rule scales the rigor of every rule to the consequence of failure
times the uncertainty, and a rigor matrix states which rules apply in which
cell. The rules were collected from 130 engineering and management domains
in 20 branches, every rule is linked to its sources, and all but one rule
rests on at least one primary source. Of the rules, 67 are restated in
management form and 16 come from management sources. The taxonomy is a
synthesis of published practice, not an empirical test of it. The paper
describes the method that produced it, reports the open limits, and releases
the taxonomy as a versioned, machine-checked dataset.
